## What Was Built

A team of 24 Grok Bot bots that runs Daniel Stroup's author business and his AI LABORATORY™ work like a staff. Every task goes to one Chief of Staff bot, which routes it to one of four section chiefs: Operations, Technology, Finance, or Media. Each chief has a Tenth Man, a designated dissenter that argues against the chief's recommendation before it reaches Daniel. Specialists sit under the chiefs, like the Managing Editor that runs the revision of *The Luminari Expanse* series. Every bot reports into one shared Master Task List, and the bots work from one shared Google Drive, so the files act as the team's memory.

## Why It Was Built

I started using Grok Bot to upgrade how I wrote my books. I was managing 12+ separate chats, each scoped to one task or project, and that had become unwieldy, and I wanted to keep expanding my AI learning. After some setup it proved more effective overall than the Claude setup I had been using, mainly because of connectors and task delegation. It also forced a redesign of how I run projects, book writing, and website updates, which had been slow and cumbersome as separate efforts.

## Starting Competence

New to the Grok Bot platform. The first specialist bot created was a Platform Expert to advise on it. Earlier AI work had used Claude in separate, task-scoped chats and a CrewAI multi-agent pipeline, but never a standing team of bots coordinating with each other.

## How AI Was Used

The design is a staff, not a set of tools. One front door: the Chief of Staff routes work and reports back with one voice. Four chiefs each file one short weekly report for a Saturday review, and each chief's Tenth Man tries to break its recommendation first, so Daniel decides instead of debugging. Each task on the shared list has an owner, a status, and a due date; an undated task gets a date or gets set aside.

The overhauls were built to expand. For the books, Book 2 was split into Books 2 and 3 with one numbered folder per book and a template for new books, then the Drive was reorganized in a 65-move file-structure revision. Bots read canon before touching a chapter, write edits back to the same files, and archive old drafts. The Managing Editor line-edits one chapter at a time: numbered findings, Daniel rules item by item, then one guarded write verified byte for byte, with every ruling logged. For the AI LABORATORY™ site, the GitHub Engineer rebuilt it on Astro with one file per case study and strict build checks, so a broken entry can't reach the live site. It added a category portfolio and moved deploys to GitHub Actions. Nothing merges without Daniel's approval.

Governance uses four layers: connector permissions chosen at sign-in, explicit rules that hold bots below what a connector allows, steps that wait for Daniel's click (sending, merging, purchasing), and total separation, since some accounts are never connected. The bots work in an AI LABORATORY™ workspace walled off from personal accounts, with gates opened on purpose.

## Timeline & Conditions

About three weeks, Oct 10 to Nov 2, 2026, alongside the Book 2 revision and the AI LABORATORY™ site work it was running. The team works within a weekly usage limit, which shaped later efficiency changes.

## Outcome & Evidence

As of Nov 2, 2026, the shared Master Task List tracks 119 work tasks, 50 of them done. On Book 2, *The Void Between*, the Prologue through ch.12 were line-edited between Oct 20 and Nov 1, 13 of 25 parts. The 65-move Drive revision finished with 0 failures and nothing deleted. On the AI LABORATORY™ site, 12 pull requests merged between Oct 29 and Nov 1, putting the Astro rebuild live. luminariexpanse.com also got its Privacy and Terms pages, a verified sending domain, and a fixed free-book page. Daniel can now run several workstreams at once and set large workloads in motion with limited supervision.

## Lessons Learned

You don't have to design the system yourself. Define the criteria, what success looks like, and the problems to avoid. Ask the bot for recommendations and make it ask you clarifying questions. Before anything is implemented, have it read everything back so you can confirm it understands the goal and has a verified structure for its approach. Have it produce visual representations so you can see the structure, not just read it. The connector permissions and the bots asking before important steps are what made the results easy to trust.
